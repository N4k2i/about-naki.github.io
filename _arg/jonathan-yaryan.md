Jonathan had always understood **Nawkies** as a name he could place between himself and the work.

The distinction collapsed when Naki called him Jonathan.

Nobody in TAHAUFYA was supposed to know that name because the story had no reason to contain it.
Yet all twenty-five knew it after crossing. They did not know every private fact. They knew something
narrower and worse: **they knew he was the one who had written them.**

Jonathan spent the first day answering questions until answers stopped helping.

Did you choose my species?  
Did you choose who I love?  
Did you know it hurt when you wrote it?  
If you deleted a scene, did we live it?  
When you changed canon, what happened to the version before?  
When you stopped writing for the night, did time stop?

Stephanie E. interrupted one spiral by putting food on the table. Jason W. wrote down practical
problems. Amy M. sat beside the fictional Amy. Shadow slept under a chair. Scout followed Naki.

Jonathan's worst realization was simple:

He had always been able to close the document.

They had never been able to close him.
