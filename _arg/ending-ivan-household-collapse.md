This ending begins with one broken house rule.

Not a supernatural glitch.

Not a failure of the doorway.

A choice.

Ivan decides the restrictions around him are proof that nobody in the house will ever see him as
anything except the incident.

He stops cooperating with the separation rules.

The first warning is not violence.

It is absence.

Jason W. notices that one of the household safety checks no longer matches the written protocol.
Stephanie E. notices Ivan has stopped answering when people speak to him. Amy M. notices Shadow and
Scout refusing to enter the hallway.

Jonathan notices the ARG has stopped logging Ivan's location.

That should have been impossible.

The site had tracked everyone.

Then the signal indicator changes:

`IVAN // POSITION UNKNOWN`

Lime immediately says everybody needs to move.

Aztec agrees.

Emily does not argue.

That is how serious it is.

Nobody reaches the doorway in time.

---

The ARG does not provide a detailed reconstruction of the killings.

It records only outcomes.

Jonathan Yaryan / Nawkies is killed.

Stephanie E. is killed.

Jason W. is killed.

Amy M. is killed.

Shadow is killed.

Scout is killed.

The household side of the bridge becomes silent.

---

The TAHAUFYA cast survives the immediate event.

That survival becomes its own punishment.

Mako reaches the room too late.

Markov is the first to understand that Scout is not getting back up.

Amy finds Amy M.'s index card on the floor.

The card still says:

`AMY M.`

She sits beside it for so long that nobody asks her to move.

Naki does not look at Jonathan's body for several minutes.

Blade stays close without trying to make him react.

Doc checks for signs of life even after the outcome is obvious because procedure is the only thing
keeping his hands steady.

Scarf says nothing.

Joel makes no joke.

Sododa does not try to be optimistic.

Polar stops speaking formally.

Redsky cries quietly.

Raine takes off the propeller hat.

Diamond's synthesizer says only:

`NO.`

Lime and Aztec stop discussing the bridge and begin discussing responsibility.

Emily looks at Ivan and, for the first time in the ARG, does not frame the situation as opposition,
procedure, or defense.

She says:

"You chose this."

Ivan says nothing.

---

The most devastating part is what happens to the signal.

It does not collapse immediately.

For eleven minutes, it becomes stronger.

`SIGNAL: 100.0000%`

Every record agrees.

Every camera agrees.

Every witness that remains agrees.

Every physical effect agrees.

Every memory agrees.

There is no contradiction left.

Jonathan is dead.

Stephanie E. is dead.

Jason W. is dead.

Amy M. is dead.

Shadow is dead.

Scout is dead.

The system has perfect continuity.

That is when Naki understands the real horror.

The signal was never measuring goodness.

It was never measuring safety.

It was never measuring whether a version deserved to survive.

It was measuring agreement.

A terrible event can produce perfect consensus.

---

The site begins rewriting itself.

The household witness layer disappears because there are no surviving household witnesses.

`WITNESS LAYER: EMPTY`

The cat-control records disappear.

`FELINE CONTROL: ZERO`

The two-Amys distinction breaks.

Every record that once said `AMY M.` begins degrading toward `AMY`.

Amy watches the letters disappear one by one.

She manually retypes them.

The site removes them again.

She types faster.

The site wins.

The Character Bible remains.

The TAHAUFYA cast remains.

The real-world side loses every person who had known them as people before the archive explained
them.

Recognition collapses back into description.

---

Jonathan's About page survives.

That becomes unbearable.

It still says he was a developer, gamer, storyteller, and worldbuilder.

It still lists projects.

It still has links.

It still contains sentences about how small ideas became enormous systems.

Naki stares at the page for a long time.

Then he edits one line.

He changes:

`Storyteller`

to:

`Was here.`

The site allows the change.

---

Mako does not let Ivan near Markov or Amy.

Lime and Aztec take over the remaining safety procedures.

Emily agrees to help contain Ivan.

No one calls it a trial.

There is no jurisdiction.

There is no household anymore.

There is only a room full of people who know exactly what happened.

Ivan finally speaks.

"I knew you would never stop seeing me as dangerous."

Naki answers:

"You proved the warning right."

Ivan says:

"Then you already decided."

Naki says:

"No. You did."

---

The doorway back to TAHAUFYA remains open for nine minutes.

Nobody wants to use it.

Leaving feels like abandoning the dead.

Staying feels like occupying their house.

The cats' food bowls are still where they were.

Jason's protocol is still open.

Stephanie's folded blanket is still on the chair.

Amy M.'s name card is still on the floor.

Jonathan's laptop is still running.

The cursor blinks.

Eventually Mako says they have to go.

Not because the real world is rejecting them.

Because nobody who belonged to the household is left to consent to their staying.

That distinction matters.

The cast returns.

Ivan is taken with them.

Nobody leaves him behind in Jonathan's world.

---

Back inside TAHAUFYA, the bridge closes.

This time it does not become `LOW / STABLE`.

It becomes:

`REAL-WORLD ENDPOINT: LOST`

`HOUSEHOLD WITNESSES: 0`

`AUTHOR: DECEASED`

`EXTERNAL RECOGNITION: FAILED`

`RETURN PATH: NONE`

The website remains online.

But no one on the real-world side knows what it means.

Visitors can still read Jonathan's profile.

They can still read the Character Bible.

They can still find the ARG.

They can still solve the puzzles.

They can still reach this ending.

But the people who lived through it are gone.

---

The last surviving real-world log was written automatically.

It contains six names:

Jonathan Yaryan.

Stephanie E.

Jason W.

Amy M.

Shadow.

Scout.

No titles.

No roles.

No descriptions.

Only names.

Beneath them:

`THEY WERE REAL TO EACH OTHER.`

Then:

`THE BRIDGE DID NOT SAVE THEM.`

Then:

`CONSENSUS IS NOT MERCY.`

---

This branch permanently changes the meaning of the signal mechanic.

Before this ending, a higher signal looked desirable.

After this ending, the player knows better.

Perfect agreement can describe a perfect tragedy.

The system can be functioning exactly as designed while everything worth preserving is gone.

The deepest rule added by this ending is:

> **Continuity is not morality.**

The archive can preserve truth.

It cannot choose what should happen.

People do that.

---

There is no code that fixes this branch.

No hidden route restores the household.

No Character Bible entry resurrects Jonathan.

No witness protocol brings back Stephanie E., Jason W., or Amy M.

No cat behavior reveals Shadow or Scout waiting somewhere unseen.

They are dead.

The failure ending remains a failure ending.

The archive does not offer a cleaner version.
