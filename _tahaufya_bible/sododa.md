E. Thought Process &amp; Decision Engine
103. 1. NOTICE - Sododa first filters a situation through optimistic: what detail feels immediately important?
104. 2. CLASSIFY - the excited side decides whether the situation is safe, funny, insulting, urgent, intimate, or strategically useful.
105. 3. PROTECT - before choosing an action, they protect a self-image connected to being energetic. This is where a flaw can hide
     inside a virtue.
106. 4. CHOOSE - they select the move most likely to help them make everything more fun without making everything about them.
     Under pressure, this short-term goal can conflict with what they actually need: learn to check whether enthusiasm is shared.
107. 5. REVISE - if another person reacts unexpectedly, the earnest trait determines whether they double down, ask a question, joke,
     retreat, repair, or change strategy.
108. 6. MEMORY - meaningful consequences should alter later choices. A relationship bible is useful only if the character remembers
     what earlier scenes taught them.

F. Voice, Dialogue &amp; Silence
BIBLE EXPANSION
Opening a conversation: Start from what they actually want rather than filler. Their first line usually establishes purpose, mood, or a
small observation.


Dodging a question: Change level instead of topic: answer the literal part while leaving the emotional part untouched, or redirect into
logistics.
Teasing: Target familiarity, not humiliation. The best tease proves they know the other person well.
Apologizing: Name the action, name the effect, avoid &#x27;if you felt&#x27;, and show the changed behavior after the apology.
Being affectionate: Use character-specific attention: remembered preferences, proximity, a shared routine, or an exact observation
rather than generic declarations every time.
Setting a boundary: Make the sentence shorter and less ornamental than normal. Important boundaries should be legible even if the
character is uncomfortable.
Lying: The lie should exploit their normal truth-telling habits. A usually detailed speaker may become too clean; a usually concise
speaker may over-explain.
Panicking: Vocabulary may simplify, but core priorities stay recognizable. Do not make panic an excuse for random behavior.
Arguing: Let them pursue a specific win condition: being understood, establishing facts, protecting someone, ending the exchange, or
forcing acknowledgment.
Flirting: Keep the character&#x27;s normal intelligence and humor intact. Flirting should sound like this person with more relational
attention, not a stock romance voice.
Court / formal pressure: Separate what they know, what they infer, and what they feel. Precision creates tension because emotional
reality does not always fit procedural language.
After silence: The line that breaks silence should reveal what they chose to prioritize during the pause.
-    Can a reader identify Sododa from a line with the name tag removed?
-    Does the line reflect at least one of: optimistic, excited, energetic - rather than generic cleverness?
-    Does the character say more, less, or different information around a partner/family member than around a stranger?
-    If the character is silent, is the silence doing something specific - refusal, processing, observation, fear, restraint, affection, or
     exhaustion?
-    Avoid catchphrases so frequent that the character turns into an imitation of themselves.
-    Let vocabulary change with context, but preserve the underlying decision-making rhythm.

I. Conflict, Boundaries &amp; Repair
-    Conflict trigger: anything that activates the fear of being the only person still excited after a line was crossed can make normal
     optimistic behavior harden into a defense.
-    Common mistake: using energetic as proof that their interpretation must be correct.
-    Escalation test: before making the character crueler, ask what they are trying to protect and why a less destructive option feels
     unavailable in that exact moment.
-    Boundary language: a real boundary must be understandable to the other person; ambiguity may be emotionally realistic, but the
     story should know when a boundary was actually communicated.
-    Repair language: apologies name behavior and effect. The next scene should contain evidence that the lesson survived the
     apology.
-    Trust is not binary. Track separate trust categories: physical safety, emotional confidentiality, reliability, judgment, honesty,
     competence, and romantic exclusivity where relevant.
-    After major harm, do not jump straight to normal banter because the plot needs the group together. Shared space can continue
     while trust remains damaged.
-    Prank-war rule: stop immediately for real injury, genuine distress, or a clear no. Cleverness never outranks safety.
