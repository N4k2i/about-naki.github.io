The second morning was worse because the sun rose normally.

Somewhere outside, people woke up, worked, laughed, forgot things, and continued living in a world
that had no idea twenty-five fictional people were trying to fit inside one household.

Normality felt crueler than catastrophe.

A catastrophe would have acknowledged them.

Jonathan opened the curtains only far enough for light.

Redsky stood in it for several minutes.

Sododa said the sky looked exactly like a sky and then apologized because they could not explain why
that was upsetting.

Mako asked when they could leave safely.

Jonathan said he did not know.

That answer became the first recurring sentence of the ARG.

## 18+ HOUSEHOLD LAYER

The mature version treats the household emergency as exhausting rather than whimsical. Privacy collapses.
People sleep badly. Arguments happen because twenty-five traumatized strangers and six household members cannot
share limited space without friction. Fear turns ordinary noises into threats. Nobody has enough information,
money, room, or emotional capacity.

Stephanie E., Jason W., and Amy M. are forced to live with consequences they never consented to when Jonathan
created the story. Jonathan knows that. The guilt becomes harder to compartmentalize every day.
