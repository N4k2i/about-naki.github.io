Jonathan wrote because the records stabilized people.

He also wrote because he was terrified.

Every page created continuity.

Every page also turned suffering into content.

Stephanie E. suggested a rule: Jonathan could record what happened, but characters could read and
correct the record before publication.

Amy M. added another: family conversations were not automatically content.

Jason W. added another: the ARG would never include real addresses or private details merely because
fictional framing made them dramatic.

Even inside a story about reality breaking, the real family deserved privacy.

## 18+ ERASURE LAYER

The erasure is more frightening in this archive. People lose seconds, then names, then the ability to trust their own
memory. Panic attacks, dissociation, rage, and helplessness become common. A character can be touching somebody's hand
while that person sincerely insists nobody is there.

The horror is not gore. It is being alive while the people who love you become neurologically unable to recognize you.
