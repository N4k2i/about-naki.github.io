Jonathan knew the name before the stranger said it.

Naki.

N4-K2.

An uncommon Expie variant. Glasses. Heterochromia. Quiet, emotionally deep, observant, creative,
anxious, loyal, slow to trust, capable of dry humor. The words arrived in Jonathan's head with the
ugly precision of a database query.

Naki flinched.

"Don't do that."

Jonathan had not spoken.

"Do what?"

"List me."

The answer changed the room.

Naki could not read Jonathan's thoughts. What he recognized was the expression of somebody retrieving
a character sheet instead of seeing a person.

Stephanie E. reached the hallway first after Jonathan. Jason W. came next, then Amy M.
Shadow stayed hidden. Scout approached two steps and stopped.

Nobody knew what to call the situation.

Naki solved that problem by asking for water.

The request was so ordinary that Jonathan almost cried.

In the kitchen, Naki held the glass with both hands and looked at every object as if it had too much
detail. The refrigerator hum. A scratch in the table. The uneven reflection on a spoon.

"None of this has narrative priority," he said.

Jonathan did not understand.

"In the story, if something gets described, it matters. Here everything exists at the same time."

Then the living-room light flickered.

Twenty-three new files appeared in the browser cache.

Naki set the glass down.

"I don't think I came alone."

## 18+ ARCHIVE LAYER

The mature archive does not soften the panic of the first breach. Jonathan's first response is not wonder.
It is the sick certainty that something has crossed a boundary he does not understand and that his family is
now inside the consequences of a project he thought could never physically touch them.

The house does not feel haunted. It feels **compromised**.

Nobody knows whether the next person through the hallway will be frightened, dangerous, injured, unstable,
or carrying a history that was only ever safe because it existed behind a screen.
