Doc noticed the correlation.

Characters whose pages were opened more often suffered fewer memory glitches.

Handwritten references helped.

Spoken names helped briefly.

The worst instability surrounded Open Fields.

The site was not simply describing them.

It was functioning as a persistence layer.

Nobody liked that.

Naki had spent days resenting the dossier's claim on him.

Now deleting the dossier might remove more than the claim.

"I can hate the cage and still not want you to remove the floor," he told Jonathan.

The phrase entered the archive.

## 18+ ERASURE LAYER

The erasure is more frightening in this archive. People lose seconds, then names, then the ability to trust their own
memory. Panic attacks, dissociation, rage, and helplessness become common. A character can be touching somebody's hand
while that person sincerely insists nobody is there.

The horror is not gore. It is being alive while the people who love you become neurologically unable to recognize you.
