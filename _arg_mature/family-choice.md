Jonathan wanted the third answer because every other option felt like admitting failure.

Stephanie E. asked whether wanting a third answer made one exist.

Jason W. asked how long the household could continue if the signal kept falling.

Amy M. asked what "continue" meant if the alternative was abandoning people already integrated into
daily life.

Jonathan called them a burden.

Stephanie corrected him.

"A burden is real. That doesn't mean you throw it away."

Jason added that helping did not mean pretending there were no limits.

Amy M. said she was not ready for the house to become normal if normal meant forgetting.

Jonathan thought about his habit of turning small ideas into enormous systems.

The joke had stopped being funny.

He had made a story.

The story had become a household emergency.

He voted third answer.

## 18+ DECISION LAYER

The vote is crueler when every option can plausibly destroy somebody. Returning means surrendering part of the freedom
they fought to prove. Staying means risking erasure. The third answer is mostly theory and could fail catastrophically.

People argue. Some accuse others of cowardice. Some apologize later. Some do not.

Consent under impossible circumstances is still consent under pressure.
