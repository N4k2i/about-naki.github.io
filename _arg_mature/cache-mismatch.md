The first image appeared in a cache folder as a corrupted rectangle: not enough pixels to identify
a face, too much structure to dismiss as noise. The browser labeled it as an asset belonging to the
character-bible page even though the repository contained no corresponding image.

Jonathan opened it in three viewers. Each rendered a different arrangement of dark shapes.
One showed two points at eye height. Another showed the reflection of Jonathan's room from an angle
no camera occupied.

Stephanie E. was the first person Jonathan showed. She did not say it was obviously nothing.
She asked the useful question instead:

"Did you make this?"

Jonathan said no.

Jason W. checked the file properties. Amy M. watched the cats. Shadow had moved under a chair.
Scout stood with his tail low and his attention fixed on the hallway.

The corrupted image changed again.

This time the pixels resolved into a pair of glasses.

Jonathan recognized them before he recognized the face.

He closed the image.

The browser reopened it by itself.

## 18+ ARCHIVE LAYER

The mature archive does not soften the panic of the first breach. Jonathan's first response is not wonder.
It is the sick certainty that something has crossed a boundary he does not understand and that his family is
now inside the consequences of a project he thought could never physically touch them.

The house does not feel haunted. It feels **compromised**.

Nobody knows whether the next person through the hallway will be frightened, dangerous, injured, unstable,
or carrying a history that was only ever safe because it existed behind a screen.
