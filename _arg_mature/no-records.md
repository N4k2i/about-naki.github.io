The real world had systems for people: names, records, accounts, photographs, proof that someone
had existed before the moment somebody asked.

The TAHAUFYA cast had none here.

Their names existed on Jonathan's website.

That was not the same as existing in the systems the real world used to decide whether somebody
belonged to it.

Lime, Aztec, and Emily spent hours discussing personhood and documentation. There was no safe
institution to approach with "twenty-five fictional people became physically real."

The depressing part was not rejection.

It was the absence of a field.

They escaped a story with too much definition into a world whose first question would be whether
they could prove they had ever been born.

## 18+ OUTSIDE LAYER

The outside-world problem becomes harsher here. No documentation means no straightforward path to employment,
healthcare, identification, housing, banking, or legal recognition. Twenty-five conscious people can be physically
present and still be functionally nonexistent to every institution that decides who counts.

The mature archive emphasizes the dread of being trapped between being *too real to be fictional* and
*too undocumented to be treated as real*.
